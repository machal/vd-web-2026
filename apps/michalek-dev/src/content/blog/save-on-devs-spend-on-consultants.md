# Save on developers, spend on consultants

Dear site owners: the more you save on platform and developers, the more you may spend on consultants cleaning up afterward.

That can still be rational. Just put the variable in your spreadsheet.

> "We'll save X on the platform and Y on developers, but budget Z for UX, SEO, accessibility, or performance consulting — and hire those people **early**, not after launch."

![What the consultant sees versus what the owner sees](https://res.cloudinary.com/vzhurudolu-cz/image/upload/v1714374394/vzhurudolu-blog/mili-provozovatele_ogqic1.jpg)

Take a solid SaaS e-commerce platform — when used as intended it can be excellent, performance included. Used as a shortcut for bespoke work, it often is not.

"We'll build on [platform X]. Look how much we save each month…" Fine — but do not expect unlimited customisation. Forced template tweaks frequently hurt UX and wreck performance.

Or **WordPress**. A classic. Yes, WordPress can be done well. Often it is not: a cheap developer who does not understand the stack, a site held together by unmaintained plugins, poor communication, and silence while they are on holiday.

At [PageSpeed.ONE](https://pagespeed.one/) we see this pattern in performance work. Audits and remediation on "budget" builds can cost more than doing it properly once. The project may still pencil out — but do the math upfront. Do not be surprised later. Consider yourself warned.
