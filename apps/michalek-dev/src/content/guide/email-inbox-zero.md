# Inbox Zero: escape email chaos

Email does not have to be evil. Your inbox does not need promos, stale threads, or that nagging feeling you forgot someone. Two exits exist: pretend email is not real, or adopt **Inbox Zero** — a system for processing mail on your terms.

## Why email still matters {#e-mail-vyhody}

Some professionals flee to Slack, Teams, or the phone for everything. That breeds forgotten decisions and searchable history scattered across tools.

Email's strengths:

- no expectation of instant reply — time to think
- searchable, durable threads
- less interruptive than IM — better for [deep work](https://www.vzhurudolu.cz/podcast/75-hluboka-prace)
- universal — everyone has an address

Email complements other channels. Used badly it feels toxic; used with a method it is manageable.

## What is Inbox Zero? {#inbox-zero}

[Inbox Zero](http://www.43folders.com/43-folders-series-inbox-zero), popularised by [Merlin Mann](http://www.merlinmann.com/) in the 2000s, aims for an inbox regularly at or near empty.

In practice:

* Touch email on a schedule — at least daily.
* Apply the four Ds: **Do, Delegate, Defer, Delete** (I add **Deter** for spam).
* Archive or delete after acting.
* End with a clear inbox and a clearer head.

I've run this roughly ten years — since the [Google Inbox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inbox_by_Gmail) era. Today I use Gmail; the client matters less than the habit.

Results: less inbox dread, fewer dropped balls, faster retrieval, saner days.

## My communication stack {#komunikace}

Context first. I consult on [web performance](https://www.vzhurudolu.cz/martin) but spend much of my week on management — [PageSpeed.ONE](https://pagespeed.one/) with ~fifty clients, a speed-testing product, [Frontendisti.cz](https://frontendisti.cz/), and writing. Roughly a hundred emails daily plus social and IM without a system would drown me.

### Email (Gmail) {#komunikace-email}

Central hub. Project notifications from Basecamp land here. I check a few times daily — notifications off.

### IM (Slack) {#komunikace-im}

Real-time team chat for urgencies and water-cooler channels. Notifications mostly afternoons unless family pings.

### Phone {#komunikace-tel}

Rare but useful for nuance after long threads. Some clients prefer calls — I adapt.

### Video calls {#komunikace-call}

Most meetings are remote now. Fine by me — though I still value occasional in-person time when we can make it happen.

## Prerequisite: protected time {#predpoklad-1}

Email costs time. Accept that.

My rhythm:

* **Morning:** no inbox — deep work.
* **After lunch:** 30–45 minutes — main Inbox Zero block, calendar-protected.
* **Afternoon:** occasional quick replies; heavy threads wait for tomorrow.

I track time in Toggl — client advisory vs internal comms vs "Communication" bucket capped around thirty minutes for non-billable mail.

Afternoon slots fit email for me: lower cognitive peak, mail rarely needs deepest focus.

### One inbox {#predpoklad-2-jedno}

Old addresses forward to my current Gmail. Promotions/Social tabs off — if it belongs in my world, it lands in one queue. ~5–10 newsletters max; social notifications never.

### Short messages {#predpoklad-2-delka}

I used to write novels (former developer habit). Now I prefer [Five.sentenc.es](http://five.sentenc.es/) discipline — five sentences when possible. [Five Ws](https://www.intercom.com/blog/how-to-send-the-perfect-message-with-the-five-ws/) help beginners structure asks.

## Daily workflow {#krok-1}

1. **Quick wins first** — Basecamp notifications, one-minute replies.
2. **Client threads** needing real answers — timed if billable.
3. **Defer** low-priority ~10% to tomorrow.

* **Delete / Archive** — remove noise; archive what you might search later.
* **Delegate** — reassign when you are not the owner (rare for me; roles are fairly clear).
* **Do** — under two minutes, do now.
* **Defer** — task manager or snooze.
* **Deter** — spam and unsubscribe without guilt.
